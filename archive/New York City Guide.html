Day 1
Lower Manhattan & Brooklyn Bridge
History · Icons · Views
☀️ Morning
9/11 Memorial & Museum
⏱ 1.5–2 h · 🏛 9AM–8PM · 📍 Fulton St or WTC Cortlandt
Start here — the reflecting pools and memorial are most peaceful in the morning before crowds arrive. The museum (separate ticket) is deeply moving; allow 90 min if you go inside.
📍 Open in Maps
Oculus / Westfield World Trade Center
⏱ 15 min · free
Step inside Santiago Calatrava's stunning white wing structure — one of NYC's most dramatic modern spaces. Worth a quick wander.
🌤 Afternoon
Walk the Brooklyn Bridge
⏱ 45 min walk · free · 📍 City Hall Park
The classic NYC experience. Walk from Manhattan to Brooklyn for spectacular skyline views — wear comfortable shoes. Cross early afternoon to avoid the evening crowd and catch the Manhattan skyline facing west on your return.
📍 Open in Maps
DUMBO & Brooklyn Heights Promenade
⏱ 1.5 h · free
After crossing, stroll through DUMBO (the cobblestone streets framing the bridge are iconic for photos), then head up to the Brooklyn Heights Promenade for arguably the best skyline view in the city.
📍 Open in Maps
🌆 Evening
Financial District & Stone Street
📍 Stone St, Manhattan
Head back to Manhattan and wander Wall Street, the New York Stock Exchange, and the old Charging Bull. Stone Street is a cobblestone pedestrian lane lined with bars — great for a first-night drink.
📍 Open in Maps
Dinner at Katz's Delicatessen
🍽 205 E Houston St · cash preferred
Open since 1888, this is the most iconic deli in NYC. Order the pastrami on rye — there's nothing else like it. The "I'll have what she's having" table from When Harry Met Sally is marked.
📍 Open in Maps
Day 2
Midtown & Central Park
Skylines · Culture · Energy
☀️ Morning
Central Park
⏱ 1.5–2 h · free · best entered at 59th St
Start with a walk through the southern end of the park. Hit Bethesda Fountain, the iconic Bow Bridge, Strawberry Fields (John Lennon memorial), and the Reservoir path. Rent a bike if you want to cover more ground.
📍 Open in Maps
Bagel breakfast
🥯 Absolute Bagels · 2788 Broadway (Upper West Side)
Grab a bagel before or after your park walk. Absolute Bagels is a neighborhood institution — get it toasted with lox cream cheese. Alternatively, Russ & Daughters (179 E Houston) is the gold standard since 1914.
🌤 Afternoon
MoMA — Museum of Modern Art
⏱ 2 h · 🏛 10:30AM–5:30PM (Fri until 9PM) · 11 W 53rd St
One of the world's great modern art collections. Highlights include Starry Night (Van Gogh), Water Lilies (Monet), and the design collection. The sculpture garden is beautiful on a clear day.
📍 Open in Maps
Top of the Rock
⏱ 1 h · 30 Rockefeller Plaza · book in advance
Rockefeller Center's observation deck with unobstructed views of the Empire State Building and Central Park — better for photography than the Empire State Building itself. The sunset slot is the best time to visit.
📍 Open in Maps
🌆 Evening
Times Square (briefly) & Theater District
📍 Broadway & 7th Ave · best at dusk
At least once: experience Times Square at dusk when the screens come alive. It's overwhelming but unmissably NYC. Then grab dinner in the Theater District or Hell's Kitchen (9th Ave has excellent, affordable restaurants).
📍 Open in Maps
Optional: Broadway show
🎭 Book in advance · TKTS booth in Times Square for same-day discount tickets
If there's a show you want to see, tonight's the natural night for it. The TKTS booth (red steps in Times Square) sells same-day tickets for 20–50% off.
Day 3
Upper East Side & Museum Mile
Art · Elegance · Old Money
☀️ Morning
The Metropolitan Museum of Art
⏱ 2.5–3 h · 🏛 10AM–5PM (Fri–Sat until 9PM) · 1000 5th Ave · suggested donation
One of the greatest museums in the world — arrive when it opens. Don't try to see everything; pick a few wings. The Egyptian wing with the Temple of Dendur, the European Paintings, and the Roof Garden (seasonal) are standouts.
📍 Open in Maps
🌤 Afternoon
The Frick Collection
⏱ 1 h · 🏛 10AM–6PM · 1 E 70th St · $30
An intimate collection of Old Masters in a gilded-age mansion — Vermeer, Rembrandt, Velázquez. One of NYC's most underrated museum experiences. The indoor courtyard is stunning.
📍 Open in Maps
Madison Avenue & Lexington Ave stroll
📍 UES, 60s–80s streets
Walk down Madison Ave past designer boutiques, then cut to Lexington Ave for the neighborhood feel — great cafes and bakeries. Stop at Sant Ambroeus (982 Madison) for a proper Italian coffee.
📍 Open in Maps
🌆 Evening
Harlem exploration
📍 125th St · take the 4/5/6 uptown
Head uptown to Harlem. Walk 125th Street (the main commercial strip), see the Apollo Theater marquee, and explore the brownstone blocks. Harlem has some of the best soul food and brunch in the city.
📍 Apollo Theater Maps
Dinner: Sylvia's Restaurant
🍽 328 Lenox Ave · Harlem · since 1962
The queen of soul food in NYC. Fried chicken, BBQ ribs, candied yams, cornbread. Sunday gospel brunch is legendary if your visit aligns. No reservations needed on weekdays.
📍 Open in Maps
Day 4
Brooklyn — Full Day
Cool · Local · Foodie
☀️ Morning
Smorgasburg (weekends only)
⏱ 11AM–6PM Sat · Prospect Park; Sun · Williamsburg Waterfront · free entry
If you're visiting on a weekend, start here. Over 100 food vendors — the best outdoor food market in NYC. The Ramen Burger and mango sticky rice are iconic. Amazing waterfront views in Williamsburg.
📍 Open in Maps
Williamsburg
📍 Bedford Ave · L train from 14th St
Brooklyn's hippest neighborhood. Bedford Ave is the main strip with vintage shops, independent bookstores, and great coffee. Grab a coffee at Toby's Estate (125 N 6th St) or Blue Bottle.
📍 Open in Maps
🌤 Afternoon
Brooklyn Museum
⏱ 1.5 h · 🏛 Wed–Sun 11AM–6PM · 200 Eastern Pkwy · $25 suggested
The second-largest art museum in the US — far less crowded than the Met. The Egyptian collection, American art, and feminist art wing (Elizabeth A. Sackler Center) are highlights.
📍 Open in Maps
Prospect Park & Brooklyn Botanic Garden
⏱ 1 h · free · 990 Washington Ave
Frederick Law Olmsted's other great park (he co-designed Central Park). The Botanic Garden next door is one of NYC's hidden gems — the Japanese Hill-and-Pond Garden is beautiful.
📍 Open in Maps
🌆 Evening
DUMBO at golden hour
📍 Washington St & Water St · most photographed street in NYC
Head to the corner of Washington St and Water St at golden hour — the Brooklyn Bridge framed between the brick buildings is the most iconic NYC photo. Then grab a drink at Time Out Market (55 Water St) with waterfront views.
📍 Open in Maps
Dinner in Williamsburg or DUMBO
🍽 Lilia (Williamsburg) · Celestine (DUMBO)
Lilia (567 Union Ave) is one of the best Italian restaurants in NYC — book well in advance. If you can't get in, Celestine at the DUMBO waterfront is a beautiful backup with Mediterranean fare and bridge views.
📍 Lilia Maps
Day 5
West Village, High Line & Chelsea
Leafy streets · Art · Perfect pace
☀️ Morning
West Village morning wander
📍 Christopher St · A/C/E/1/2/3 to 14th St
The West Village is NYC's most charming neighborhood — narrow cobblestone streets, brownstones draped in ivy, no grid. Get lost here. Bleecker St, Christopher St, and the streets around Carmine St are the best for strolling.
📍 Open in Maps
Breakfast at Buvette
🍳 42 Grove St · West Village · 7AM–12AM daily
A tiny, perfect Parisian-style gastrotheque. The best breakfast in the West Village — avocado toast, eggs, and exceptional coffee. It's small so arrive right when it opens or be prepared to wait a bit.
📍 Open in Maps
🌤 Afternoon
The High Line
⏱ 1.5 h · free · runs from Gansevoort St to 34th St
A repurposed elevated freight railway turned linear park with rotating art installations, landscaping, and great views over the Hudson and the city. Walk it south to north — it ends near Hudson Yards. Best mid-afternoon when the light is good.
📍 Open in Maps
Chelsea Market & Chelsea Galleries
📍 75 9th Ave · Mon–Sat 7AM–9PM
Chelsea Market is a former Nabisco factory turned gourmet food hall — pick up snacks, browse the vendors. Then walk the surrounding blocks of Chelsea for free world-class contemporary art galleries (W 22nd–26th St between 10th–11th Ave).
📍 Open in Maps
🌆 Evening
The Edge Observation Deck (sunset)
⏱ 1 h · 30 Hudson Yards · book in advance · $40+
The highest outdoor observation deck in the Western Hemisphere, with a glass-floored suspended platform. Book the sunset slot — views of the Manhattan skyline, Hudson River, and New Jersey at dusk are breathtaking. A fitting end to the trip.
📍 Open in Maps
Final dinner: SoHo or West Village
🍽 L'Artusi · Via Carota · Don Angie
L'Artusi (228 W 10th St) and Via Carota (51 Grove St) are perennial West Village favorites — Italian, excellent pasta, no reservations at Via Carota so arrive by 6PM or expect a wait. Don Angie (103 Greenwich Ave) is more upscale Italian and takes reservations.
📍 Via Carota Maps
Last-night tip: After dinner, walk along the Hudson River Greenway near Pier 45 in the West Village. On a clear night the New Jersey lights reflected on the water, with the city humming behind you, is quintessential New York.

Practical Info

Subway Get an OMNY card (tap to pay) or a 7-day MetroCard ($34). The subway runs 24/7. Most attractions are within a few blocks of a station.
Taxi & Rideshare Yellow cabs are metered; hail them on the street. Uber/Lyft work well. Avoid during rush hour — it's often faster to walk or take the subway.
Neighborhoods map Lower Manhattan → Midtown → Upper East Side → Brooklyn → West Village. Each day roughly stays in one area to minimize transit.
Booking tips Reserve Top of the Rock, The Edge, 9/11 Museum, and any popular restaurant at least 2–4 weeks in advance. Broadway shows: book via Telecharge or TKTS for day-of discounts.
Walking NYC is extremely walkable. Many of the best moments happen while walking between places — build in extra time to wander.
Weather Spring (Apr–Jun) and fall (Sep–Nov) are ideal. Summers are hot and humid (30°C+). Winters can be cold but the city is magical in the snow.
Safety NYC is very safe for tourists in the main neighborhoods. Standard city precautions: be aware of your surroundings on the subway, keep your phone in your pocket.
Tipping 18–20% at restaurants (the iPad will suggest 22/25/30% — 18–20% is completely fine). $1–2/drink at bars, $2–5 for taxis.
