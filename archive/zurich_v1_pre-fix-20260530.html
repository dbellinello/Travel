Day 1 — Old Town
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 10 min · 🚕 4 min → Grossmünster
1.Grossmünster
📒 Zurich's defining landmark — a Romanesque twin-towered cathedral that anchors the east bank of the Limmat and gave birth to the Swiss Reformation under Ulrich Zwingli. Climb the Karlsturm for one of the best 360° views over the old city, the river, and on clear days the Alps.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 Karlsturm tower climb · grossmuenster.ch
🏛 Mar–Oct Mon–Sat 10am–6pm · Sun 12:30pm–6pm
🏛 Nov–Feb Mon–Sat 10am–5pm · Sun 12:30pm–5pm
⚠️ Tower restoration ongoing 2026–2027 — confirm access before visit
⏰ ~1 hr
📍 Grossmünsterplatz · Altstadt
🚶 3 min · 🚕 3 min → Fraumünster
2.Fraumünster
📒 A 9th-century convent church transformed into one of Europe's most extraordinary stained-glass interiors. Marc Chagall designed five towering chancel windows in 1970 — each 10 metres high, each a different dominant color, depicting a Biblical narrative in luminous jewel tones. Augusto Giacometti's rose window completes the north transept.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 Fraumünster entry · fraumuenster.ch · CHF 5 at door
🏛 Apr–Oct daily 10am–6pm
🏛 Nov–Mar daily 10am–4pm
⏰ ~45 min
📍 Münsterhof 2 · Altstadt
🚶 4 min · 🚕 3 min → Lindenhof
3.Lindenhof
📒 A quiet hilltop square with nearly 2,000 years of history — a Roman fort stood here in 15 BC, and a Carolingian imperial palace followed in the 9th century. Today a tranquil public garden where locals play chess under linden trees, with a wide view down to the Limmat, across to the Grossmünster, and deep into the Old Town rooftops.
📖 Wikipedia
📍 Lindenhof · Altstadt
🏛 Open 24/7
🆓 Free
⏰ ~30 min
🚶 6 min · 🚕 4 min → Niederdorf
4.Niederdorf
📒 The medieval quarter north of the Limmat — a dense network of pedestrianized alleys, guild houses, and open-front cafés that has been the social heart of Zurich's Old Town for centuries. Walk Niederdorfstrasse and its side lanes in the late afternoon when the terraces fill up: wine bars, bakeries, historic restaurants, and a continuous hum that peaks well into the night.
📖 Wikipedia
📍 Niederdorfstrasse · Altstadt
🏛 Open 24/7
🆓 Free
⏰ ~1 hr 30 min
🚶 8 min · 🚕 4 min → hotel
Day 2 — Art & Science
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 7 min · 🚕 3 min → Swiss National Museum
1.Swiss National Museum
📒 The largest historical museum in Switzerland occupies a neo-Gothic castle built in 1898 directly beside the main train station. Six permanent exhibitions trace Swiss history from the Stone Age to the present — arms collections, medieval artifacts, decorative arts, and the full sweep of Swiss cultural identity. Allow at least two hours.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 Swiss National Museum admission · landesmuseum.ch
🏛 Tue–Sun 10am–5pm · Thu 10am–7pm
🚫 Closed Monday
⏰ ~2 hrs
📍 Museumstrasse 2 · Altstadt
🚶 18 min · 🚕 7 min → ETH Poly Terrasse
2.ETH Poly Terrasse
📒 The stone terrace of ETH Zürich's main building sits on a wooded hill above the Kunsthaus and delivers Zurich's finest free panorama — the full span of the Old Town below, Lake Zurich stretching south, and the Alps as a white backdrop on clear days. Reach it via the historic Polybahn funicular from Central station (departures every 3 minutes, running since 1889).
📖 Wikipedia
📍 Rämistrasse 101 · Hochschulen
🏛 Open 24/7
🆓 Free
⏰ ~30 min
🚶 5 min · 🚕 3 min → Kunsthaus Zürich
3.Kunsthaus Zürich
📒 Switzerland's largest art museum and one of the finest in Central Europe — Monet, Van Gogh, Picasso, Giacometti (the largest collection of his sculptures anywhere), and a major modern wing completed in 2021 that doubled the gallery space. Budget at least two hours; plan around the Giacometti Foundation rooms, which are without equal.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 Kunsthaus Zürich admission · kunsthaus.ch
🏛 Tue–Wed, Fri–Sun 10am–6pm · Thu 10am–8pm
🚫 Closed Monday
⏰ ~2 hrs

Day 3 — Lake Zurich
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 9 min · 🚕 4 min → ZSG Lake Zurich Cruise
1.ZSG Lake Zurich Cruise
📒 The Zürichsee-Schifffahrtsgesellschaft has operated steamships and motor vessels on Lake Zurich since 1890. The long round-trip to Rapperswil (4.5 hrs) passes vineyard slopes, orchard villages, and opens wide Alpine views on clear days. The 90-minute cruise to Thalwil is the right choice for a half-day pairing with the Chinese Garden. Covered by the Swiss Travel Pass.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 ZSG lake cruise · zsg.ch · covered by Swiss Travel Pass · 20% off with Zürich Card
🏛 Daily Apr–Oct (reduced service Nov–Mar)
⏰ ~1 hr 30 min – 4 hrs 30 min depending on route
📍 Bürkliplatz · Enge
🚶 18 min · 🚕 7 min → Chinese Garden of Zurich
2.Chinese Garden of Zurich
📒 A gift from Zürich's partner city Kunming in 1994, this classical Chinese garden on the Zürichhorn lakefront is among the finest of its kind in Europe. Dragon-backed walls, lacquered pavilions, a koi pond, and a tea house sit within two enclosed acres — a complete tonal contrast to the Swiss city outside its gates.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 Chinese Garden admission · zuerich.com · CHF 4 adults at door
🏛 Daily 11am–7pm
🚫 Closed Nov–Mar (seasonal: Mar 18 – Oct 18 only)
⏰ ~1 hr

Day 4 — South Zürich
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚕 20 min → Lindt Home of Chocolate
1.Lindt Home of Chocolate
📒 A genuinely immersive chocolate museum anchored by the world's largest Lindt chocolate fountain — a nine-metre flowing tower of couverture that greets you at the door. The museum traces 175 years of Swiss chocolate-making with interactive exhibits, tasting stations, and the largest Lindt shop on the planet. Reserve tickets online; walk-ins often cannot get in on busy days.
📖 Wikipedia

2.Museum Rietberg
📒 One of Europe's most important non-European art museums, housed in the 19th-century Villa Wesendonck — where Wagner composed Tristan und Isolde — in the sculpted parkland of Rieterpark. Collections span Asia, Africa, the Americas, and Oceania with exceptional depth: Indian Chola bronzes, Chinese jade, Japanese Nō masks, Aztec ceramics. The underground Smaragd extension adds three further exhibition halls beneath the park.

Day 5 — Uetliberg
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 8 min → Zürich HB · 🚎 S10 25 min → Uetliberg
1.Uetliberg
📒 Zurich's home mountain at 870 metres — reachable in 25 minutes on the S10 from Hauptbahnhof. A viewing tower at the summit delivers a panorama spanning the full length of Lake Zurich, the city grid below, and on clear mornings the entire Alpine chain from Säntis to the Bernese Oberland. Arrive early to beat cloud build-up; the summit restaurant opens at 8am.
📖 Wikipedia
📍 Uetliberg Summit · Uetliberg
🏛 Open 24/7 (S10 train runs daily 05:35–00:05)
🆓 Covered by ZVV day pass or Swiss Travel Pass
⏰ ~1 hr at summit
🚶 90 min → Felsenegg (Planet Trail ridge hike)
2.Felsenegg
📒 The endpoint of the Planet Trail — a 7.5 km ridge walk from Uetliberg that passes scale models of the solar system embedded in the forest path. Felsenegg at 805 metres has its own panoramic restaurant terrace and is the top station of the Luftseilbahn Adliswil–Felsenegg cable car, which drops you to Adliswil in five minutes for the tram back to the city.
📖 Wikipedia

🏛 Cable car daily 8am–8pm (summer) · 8am–6pm (winter)

Day 6 — Café & Garden
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 5 min · 🚕 3 min → Confiserie Sprüngli
1.Confiserie Sprüngli
📒 The most famous address in Swiss confectionery — founded at Paradeplatz in 1836, still in the same family, still making Luxemburgerli (Sprüngli's signature sandwich macarons) fresh each morning. The upstairs café is a Zurich institution: marble tables, pastry trolleys, and a ringside view over the city's financial heart. Arrive before 10am on weekdays to avoid the queue.
📖 Wikipedia
📍 Bahnhofstrasse 21 · Altstadt
🏛 Mon–Fri 8:30am–6:30pm · Sat–Sun 9am–5pm

2.University Botanical Garden
📒 The University of Zurich's botanical garden on Zollikerstrasse holds more than 9,000 plant species across outdoor beds and three large climate-controlled glasshouses — tropical rainforest, succulent desert, and Victorian fern house. Beautifully maintained and entirely free; far less visited than the main tourist circuit, it rewards an unhurried afternoon.
📖 Wikipedia

1.Rhine Falls
📒 The most powerful waterfall in Europe — 150 metres wide, 23 metres high, with up to 600 cubic metres of water per second in summer. Walk the full network of viewing platforms on both banks; take the short boat ride to the central rock for a view straight up into the cascade. Schloss Laufen on the south bank has the best elevated viewpoint.
📖 Wikipedia

2.Stein am Rhein
📒 One of Switzerland's best-preserved medieval towns — the Rathausplatz is ringed by half-timbered houses whose entire façades are covered in painted murals depicting guild histories, biblical scenes, and heraldic ornaments. The cobblestoned square is unchanged since the 16th century. Walk along the Rhine and visit the medieval Kloster St. Georgen if open.

1.Chapel Bridge
📒 The Kapellbrücke is the most photographed bridge in Switzerland and the oldest surviving wooden covered bridge in Europe — built in 1333, still spanning the Reuss, decorated with 17th-century painted panels depicting Lucerne's history. Walk its full 204-metre length and look down into the clear green river. The octagonal Water Tower beside it dates to 1300.

2.Lion Monument
📒 Carved directly from the sandstone cliff in 1820 to a design by Bertel Thorvaldsen, the Löwendenkmal commemorates the 786 Swiss Guards who died defending the Tuileries in 1792. Mark Twain called it "the most mournful and moving piece of stone in the world." The dying lion lies at 10 metres long in a cliff alcove above a reflecting pool.
📖 Wikipedia

1.Old City of Berne
📒 The UNESCO-listed medieval capital of Switzerland is defined by six kilometres of arcaded sandstone colonnades — the Lauben — that line nearly every street so the city is walkable in any weather. The Zytglogge astronomical clock tower (1530), the Münster cathedral, the Bundeshaus, and the painted fountains of the Gerechtigkeitsgasse make the circuit one of the finest historic walks in Central Europe.
📖 Wikipedia

2.Bear Park
📒 Berne takes its name from the bear (Bär) and has kept live bears since the Middle Ages. The current Bärenpark — open since 2009 below the Nydeggbrücke — gives four brown bears a forested hillside with a natural stream and an Aare bathing pool. Open-air and free; bears often swim in the river on warm days, viewable from the pedestrian bridge above.

3.Rose Garden
📒 The Rosengarten sits on the ridge above the Old Town's Aare loop, and the panorama from its terrace is the classic Berne postcard — medieval rooftops and the Münster spire curving inside a broad river bend, the Bernese Alps beyond. More than 220 rose varieties bloom from late May through October; a restaurant, playground, and fountain complete the hillside park.

1.Interlaken
📒 The town between two lakes — Thun and Brienz — with an unobstructed forward view of the Eiger, Mönch, and Jungfrau that stops you cold when you first see it. Walk the Höheweg promenade from the Ost station and find the framed three-peak view from the Kursaal gardens — still the most arresting valley-floor Alpine panorama in Europe. Brief stop before Grindelwald.

2.Grindelwald First
📒 The gondola from Grindelwald village to First (2,168 m) is a 25-minute ascent through increasingly vertiginous terrain, finishing with a direct facing view of the Eiger North Face. At the top: the First Cliff Walk (a cantilever walkway bolted into the cliffside), the First Flyer zipline, and easy trails to Bachalpsee lake with its mirror-image reflection of the Schreckhorn. Swiss Travel Pass gives 50% off the gondola.

🍮 Local Tastes
Züri Geschnetzeltes
Zurich's signature dish — thinly sliced veal with mushrooms in a cream-white wine sauce served on crisp rösti. Invented in Zurich and only truly found here. Order it at any traditional Beiz in the Old Town.
📍 Zeughauskeller · Swiss Chuchi · Kronenhalle
Luxemburgerli
Sprüngli's proprietary bite-sized macaron — lighter and more delicate than the French original, with Swiss cream fillings in 20+ flavors. Made fresh daily; keeps only 2 days; never exported. The definitive Zurich edible souvenir.
📍 Confiserie Sprüngli · Paradeplatz
Fondue & Raclette
Switzerland's most iconic communal dishes — melted Gruyère swirled with wine and kirsch served with bread and potatoes; or raclette scraped over new potatoes and gherkins. Both served year-round in Zurich despite the traditional autumn–spring season.
📍 Le Dézaley · Swiss Chuchi
Rösti
The Swiss hash brown — grated potato fried crisp and golden, served as a standalone dish or as the canonical side for Züri Geschnetzeltes. Order it anywhere that says Beiz in the name.
📍 Zeughauskeller · Swiss Chuchi
Swiss Wine — Chasselas & Valais Pinot Noir
Switzerland exports almost no wine — the finest bottles stay home. Chasselas (Fendant) is the white: dry, mineral, underestimated. Valais Pinot Noir and Cornalin are the reds worth knowing. Order by the glass at any decent Zürich wine bar; you won't find the same producers outside Switzerland.
📍 Kronenhalle · Le Dézaley

Uber operates in Zürich. Swiss Cabs and Taxi 444 are the main local operators. Uber is generally cheaper — hail from the standard Uber app.
🚎 Tram
Zürich has one of Europe's most comprehensive tram networks, operated by VBZ. Trams run every 6–12 minutes on most lines from 05:30 to 00:30 daily. Key lines for this guide: Tram 3 (Hauptbahnhof–Klusplatz for ETH via Polybahn); Tram 4/15 (Hauptbahnhof–Tiefenbrunnen for Seefeld and Chinese Garden); Tram 7 (Hauptbahnhof–Wollishofen for Museum Rietberg). Buy tickets at platform machines before boarding. The Zürich Card covers all VBZ trams, buses, S-Bahn, and ZSG lake boats within the city zone.

⛲️ Day Trips by Train
Luzern — 41 min
🗞️ Chapel Bridge (Europe's oldest surviving wooden bridge), the Lion Monument, and the intact medieval Museggmauer walls. Take the cable car up Pilatus or Mount Rigi for all-day Alpine excursions. Trains run every 30 minutes from Zürich HB.
🎫 book at: sbb.ch
Bern — 57 min
🗞️ UNESCO-listed capital with six kilometres of arcaded walkways, the Zytglogge astronomical clock, the Bear Park along the Aare, and the Rose Garden panorama above the old town. Trains every 30 minutes.
🎫 book at: sbb.ch
Rhine Falls + Stein am Rhein — 47 min
🗞️ Europe's most powerful waterfall at Neuhausen am Rheinfall (boat ride to the central rock), then 20 minutes by S9 to Stein am Rhein — one of the finest intact medieval towns in Central Europe with completely painted Renaissance guild-house façades.
🎫 book at: sbb.ch
Basel — 53 min
🗞️ Switzerland's art capital — Art Basel city, the Kunstmuseum Basel (one of Europe's oldest public art collections), and a beautifully preserved Rhine waterfront. The old town pedestrian core is entirely walkable from SBB station.
🎫 book at: sbb.ch
Interlaken — 2 hrs
🗞️ Gateway to the Jungfrau region — the Höheweg promenade with direct Eiger/Mönch/Jungfrau views, with onward connections to Grindelwald First (gondola, cliff walk) and Jungfraujoch (3,454m, "Top of Europe"). Allow a full day.
🎫 book at: sbb.ch
Rapperswil — 40 min
🗞️ The "City of Roses" on the southern shore of Lake Zurich — a medieval castle above rose gardens, a compact hilltop old town, and lakefront cafés. Reachable by train or by ZSG lake boat (3-hour scenic route). Easy half-day.
🎫 book at: sbb.ch
St. Gallen — 1 hr
🗞️ Home of the UNESCO-listed Abbey District — the Cathedral and the Stiftsbibliothek Abbey Library, one of the world's oldest libraries with an astonishing Baroque interior and manuscripts from the 8th century.
🎫 book at: sbb.ch

🏔️ On Zürich

Zürich confounds first expectations. You arrive primed for a stiff, expensive banking city and instead find a place that has spent decades turning itself into one of the most liveable, most beautiful small cities in Europe. The lake is cleaner than a swimming pool. The trams run to the second. The old town is genuinely medieval. And then you turn a corner into a Chagall interior, or ride a century-old funicular up to a university terrace with the Alps spread out in front of you, and the city reveals itself properly.

The best version of Zürich is slow Zürich — the version you get when you stop trying to tick it off. Sit at a Niederdorf terrace at 6pm when the after-work crowd fills the square. Take the S10 on a weekday morning when the mist is still on the mountain and you have the Uetliberg tower almost to yourself. Find the table by the window at Zeughauskeller on a Tuesday night. The city rewards those who stay long enough to let it breathe.
