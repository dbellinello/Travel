Day 1
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 4 min · 🚕 3 min → Museo del Prado
1.Museo del Prado
📒 The world's foremost collection of Spanish painting — and one of the great encyclopedic museums on earth. Velázquez's Las Meninas, Goya's Saturn Devouring His Son, Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights, Titian and Rubens fill room after room of the neoclassical Villanueva building. Give it the morning.
📖 Wikipedia

2.Parque del Retiro & Crystal Palace
📒 Madrid's great green lung — 125 hectares of gardens and woodland with a rowing lake and the Palacio de Cristal at its center: a soaring iron-and-glass conservatory from 1887 that now hosts contemporary art installations for the Reina Sofía. Walk to the lake first then find the Crystal Palace through the trees.

Day 2
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 22 min · 🚕 8 min → Palacio Real
1.Palacio Real
📒 The largest palace in Western Europe by floor area — 135,000 m² built by the Bourbons in the 18th century on the site of Madrid's medieval alcázar. The Throne Room, the Gasparini Room and the Gala Dining Hall rank among the most opulent interiors in Europe. No longer a royal residence — open daily for visits.
📖 Wikipedia

2.Jardines de Sabatini
📒 The formal neoclassical gardens on the north face of the Royal Palace — terraced parterres, clipped hedgerows and a reflecting pool with a direct view to the palace facade and the Sierra de Guadarrama beyond. Designed on the site of the Royal Stables by the Italian architect Francesco Sabatini in the 1930s. One of the finest garden viewpoints in Madrid.
📖 Wikipedia
🆓 Free
🏛️ Daily 9am - 9pm
⏰ ~30 min

🚶 10 min · 🚕 5 min → Plaza Mayor
3.Plaza Mayor
📒 The great enclosed square at the heart of Habsburg Madrid — completed in 1619 and ringed by uniform arcaded buildings under blue slate spires. For two centuries it staged royal pageants and bullfights. The equestrian statue of Philip III presides at its center. Walk the arcades and look up at the painted facades of the Casa de la Panadería.
📖 Wikipedia
🏛️ Open 24/7
⏰ ~1 hr
📍 Plaza Mayor · Centro
🚶 23 min · 🚕 9 min → Templo de Debod
4.Templo de Debod
📒 An authentic Egyptian temple from the 2nd century BC — dismantled stone by stone when the Aswan High Dam threatened to flood it and gifted by Egypt to Spain in 1968. It stands in the Parque de la Montaña reflected in a small pool above a terrace with a wide view to the Sierra de Guadarrama. Come in the late afternoon when the light is extraordinary.
📖 Wikipedia

Day 3
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 12 min · 🚕 5 min → Museo Reina Sofía
1.Museo Reina Sofía
📒 Spain's national museum of modern art — and the permanent home of Picasso's Guernica. The painting takes up an entire wall of Room 206 and commands silence. Beyond it: Dalí's dreamscapes and Miró; a collection that maps Spanish art from the Civil War through the Transition. The converted 18th-century hospital with Jean Nouvel's glass elevator towers is worth a slow look.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟️ Museo Reina Sofía admission · museoreinasofia.es

2.Palacio de Cibeles
📒 Madrid's city hall and one of its most spectacular buildings — designed by Antonio Palacios between 1905 and 1918 with soaring white stone towers that define the intersection of Paseo del Prado and the Gran Vía. Take the elevator to the Mirador Madrid observation deck: 360° views over rooftops to the Retiro and the Guadarrama mountains.
📖 Wikipedia

3.Círculo de Bellas Artes
📒 A cultural society founded in 1880 and housed in a magnificent Art Nouveau building on Calle de Alcalá. Pay the small rooftop admission and ride up to the Azotea: a terrace with sweeping views down the Gran Vía toward the sunset and across the rooftops of 20th-century Madrid. One of the finest viewpoints in the city.
📖 Wikipedia

4.Gran Café de Gijón
📒 Opened in 1888 and recently reopened under Cappuccino Grand Café after a renovation that pledges to preserve its identity as Madrid's last literary café. Lorca, Machado and Camilo José Cela all spent time here. The long marble bar and the terrace on the Paseo de Recoletos make it unlike any other café in the city.

🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 5 min · 🚕 3 min → Museo Thyssen-Bornemisza
1.Museo Thyssen-Bornemisza
📒 The third pillar of Madrid's Golden Triangle of Art — and arguably its most accessible. The Thyssen collection spans 700 years of Western painting in one coherent sweep: Dürer, Caravaggio, Rubens, Degas, Van Gogh, Hopper and Mondrian. The Villahermosa Palace houses it all in daylit galleries that reward wandering.
📖 Wikipedia

2.Real Jardín Botánico
📒 One of the finest botanical gardens in Europe — established in 1781 by Charles III on a terraced site directly south of the Prado. Three levels of formal parterres descend from the upper terrace to the greenhouse pavilions at the base. Over 5,000 species fill the garden including a plane tree from the original planting.

1.Catedral Primada de Toledo
📒 The Primate of All Spain — the most important cathedral in the country and one of the masterworks of Gothic architecture in Europe. Built between 1226 and 1493 on the site of a Visigothic basilica. The sacristy alone contains works by El Greco, Caravaggio, Goya and Velázquez. The Transparente — a Baroque altarpiece lit by a hole cut through the vault — is one of the most audacious gestures in Spanish art.
📖 Wikipedia

2.Alcázar de Toledo
📒 A fortress on Toledo's highest point since Roman times — rebuilt by Charles V in the 16th century and famously besieged in the Civil War. The reconstructed building now houses the Army Museum. Walk around the exterior first for the panorama over the Tagus River and the terracotta rooftops of the old city before going in.

🍮 Local Tastes
Cocido Madrileño
Madrid's defining winter dish — a slow-cooked chickpea stew served in three courses: broth first, then chickpeas and vegetables, then the meats. Originally a poor man's pot, now a Sunday institution.
📍 Lhardy · La Bola Taberna · Malacatín
Churros con Chocolate
Long ridged fritters from hot oil served with a cup of thick hot chocolate for dipping. The canonical late-night Madrid meal — one of the great things to eat anywhere at 3am.
📍 Chocolatería San Ginés · Chocolatería 1902
Bocadillo de Calamares
A Madrid original — rings of fried squid piled into a crusty baguette, served plain or with alioli. Found on every bar in the city center; best near the Plaza Mayor. Simple and cheap.
📍 Bar La Campana · El Brillante
El Vermú
Sunday vermouth is a Madrid ritual — mid-morning house vermouth from the barrel with olives, anchovies and chips, taken standing at the bar before Sunday lunch. Barrio de las Letras is the best place to join it.
📍 La Venencia · Casa Alberto · El Tempranillo
Jamón Ibérico de Bellota
The finest cured ham in the world — from free-range black Iberian pigs finished on acorns in the dehesa woodlands. A single slice at a counter in Madrid is one of the finest things you can eat anywhere.
📍 Museo del Jamón · Casa González

🌞 The City That Stays Up

Madrid operates on a schedule that doesn't match anywhere else in Europe. Lunch runs 2pm to 4pm, dinner rarely starts before 9:30pm, and bars don't fill until midnight. This isn't a cultural affectation — it's partly a legacy of aligning Spain's clocks with Central European Time while the country geographically belongs in the Atlantic zone. Madrid's sun sets an hour later than you'd expect. The city took the gift seriously.

The three great museums — Prado, Reina Sofía, Thyssen — form a triangle of roughly 600 meters. Together they hold more great paintings per square kilometer than anywhere else on earth outside the Vatican. The Prado alone has over 8,000 works and shows only 1,500 of them. Visit once a day. Visit the same room twice. The paintings reward it.
