Day 1

🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 15 min · 🚕 7 min → Stanley Park
🎒 Stanley Park
📒 A 405-hectare old-growth rainforest at the tip of the downtown peninsula — home to 10 First Nations totem poles at Brockton Point, a 22 km seawall, and Lost Lagoon. Walk the waterfront section east from the Coal Harbour entrance to reach the totems at Brockton Point, then continue the seawall loop as far as time allows.
📖 Wikipedia
🏛 Open 24/7
⏰ ~3 hr
🆓 Free
📍 Stanley Park · West End
🚶 20 min · 🚕 8 min → Vancouver Lookout
🎟 Vancouver Lookout
📒 Observation deck at the top of Harbour Centre, 169 m above street level. A glass-sided elevator rises in 40 seconds; the deck offers unobstructed 360° views of downtown, Stanley Park, Burrard Inlet, and the North Shore mountains. Admission is good all day — come back for sunset.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 All-Day Admission · vancouverlookout.com
🏛 Daily 10:00am - 6:00pm
⏰ ~45 min
📍 555 W Hastings Street · Downtown
No pictures found.
🚶 5 min · 🚕 5 min → Gastown
🎒 Gastown
📒 Vancouver's oldest neighbourhood — 14 cobblestone blocks of Victorian red-brick buildings. The Steam Clock at Water and Cambie streets chimes every 15 minutes on steam power. Named after "Gassy Jack" Deighton, who set up a saloon here in 1867 and sparked the city's founding.
📖 Wikipedia
🏛 Open 24/7
⏰ ~1 hr 30 min
🆓 Free
📍 305 Water Street · Gastown

Day 2

🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚕 25 min → Capilano Suspension Bridge Park
🎟 Capilano Suspension Bridge Park
📒 A 140 m suspension bridge stretching 70 m above the Capilano River — first built in 1889, reconstructed since. The park also includes Cliffwalk (cantilevered platforms along a granite cliff face) and Treetop Adventure (seven platforms linked through 1,300-year-old Douglas firs).
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 All-Day Admission · capbridge.com
🏛 Daily 8:30am - 8:00pm
⏰ ~2 hr
⚠️ Timed entry required — book online before visiting. Walk-up slots sell out on summer weekends.
📍 3735 Capilano Road · North Vancouver
🚶 20 min · 🚕 5 min → Grouse Mountain
🎟 Grouse Mountain
📒 The Skyride gondola (new 2024 edition) climbs 1,100 m in 8 minutes to 1,231 m above sea level. At the summit: panoramic views over downtown, the Strait of Georgia, and on clear days Mount Baker. Two resident grizzly bears — Grinder and Coola — live on the summit along with hiking trails, a lumberjack show, and a raptor ridge.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 Skyride Mountain Admission · grousemountain.com
🏛 Daily 8:00am - 10:00pm
⏰ ~3 hr
⚠️ Grouse Grind trail is one-way uphill only — take the paid gondola down after hiking. No free descent.
📍 6400 Nancy Greene Way · North Vancouver
🚕 25 min → hotel

Day 3

🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚕 50 min → Shannon Falls Provincial Park
🎒 Shannon Falls Provincial Park
📒 British Columbia's third-highest waterfall at 335 m — a short 5-minute flat walk from the car park delivers you to the full curtain of falls dropping through a Douglas fir canyon. The park sits immediately beside the Sea to Sky Gondola, making the two a natural pairing in one stop.
📖 Wikipedia
🏛 Daily 7:00am - 10:00pm
⏰ ~30 min
🆓 Free
📍 Highway 99 · Squamish
🚶 10 min · 🚕 3 min → Sea to Sky Gondola
🎟 Sea to Sky Gondola
📒 An 820 m gondola climb above Howe Sound fjord to Summit Lodge at 885 m, where a suspension bridge crosses a canyon and trails fan out above the treeline. The view from the top — water, fjord walls, and peaks in every direction — is one of the most dramatic in the Sea to Sky corridor.
📖 Wikipedia

Day 4

🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚕 12 min → VanDusen Botanical Garden
🎟 VanDusen Botanical Garden
📒 Twenty-two hectares of botanic garden on a former golf course, featuring over 7,500 plant species from around the world. The Elizabethan hedge maze, rhododendron walk, and Korean Pavilion are the anchors. Established in 1975 on a site saved from development by a public campaign.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 General Admission · vandusengarden.org
🏛 Daily 9:00am - 7:00pm
⏰ ~2 hr
📍 5251 Oak Street · Shaughnessy
🚶 15 min · 🚕 5 min → Bloedel Conservatory
🎟 Bloedel Conservatory
📒 A triodetic dome 35 m wide sits atop Queen Elizabeth Park at the city's highest point (152 m), housing three climate zones — tropical rainforest, sub-tropical, and semi-arid — with over 100 free-flying tropical birds and 500+ exotic plants. The park itself provides one of the best 360° city views in Vancouver.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 General Admission · vancouver.ca
🏛 Daily 10:00am - 7:00pm
⏰ ~1 hr
📍 4600 Cambie Street · Mount Pleasant
🚕 15 min → Dr. Sun Yat-Sen Classical Chinese Garden
🎟 Dr. Sun Yat-Sen Classical Chinese Garden
📒 The first full-scale classical Chinese garden built outside China. Completed in 1986 by 52 Suzhou artisans using no power tools, it recreates a Ming Dynasty scholar's garden — Taihu limestone, black pine, bamboo, and pavilions reflected in a jade-green pool fed by a natural spring.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟 Garden Admission · vancouverchinesegarden.com
🏛 Tuesday - Sunday 9:30am - 4:00pm
🚫 Closed Monday
⏰ ~1 hr
📍 578 Carrall Street · Chinatown

🍮 Local Tastes

Pacific Salmon
Wild sockeye, coho, and chinook from BC waters — cedar-plank grilling is the defining preparation. The wild salmon fishery is Vancouver's most defining culinary ingredient, best in summer and fall.
📍 Ancora Waterfront Dining · The Fish House at Stanley Park
Spot Prawns
BC's prized local crustacean — sweet, delicate, and nothing like frozen shrimp. Wild-caught from local fjords May to July; many restaurants put them on the menu the day the boats come in.
📍 Granville Island Public Market
Dim Sum
Vancouver's Cantonese dim sum is considered by many to rival Hong Kong's — the Chinese-Canadian community has been refining it here since the 1880s. Go on a weekend morning for the full cart service.
📍 Kirin Seafood Restaurant · Sun Sui Wah
Dungeness Crab
West Coast icon — Pacific Dungeness crab is sweeter and meatier than Atlantic varieties. Best boiled whole and cracked at the table, dipped in drawn butter. Peak season October through December.
📍 Blue Water Cafe

❗ Cities Gotchas

Capilano Suspension Bridge — Timed Entry Sells Out
Walk-up admission is not available on summer weekends — timed entry slots sell out days in advance. Book online before arriving at the park.
Workaround: Buy timed entry tickets at capbridge.com at least 48 hours before your visit.
Grouse Mountain — Grouse Grind Is One-Way Up Only
The Grouse Grind trail runs uphill only. After hiking up, you must pay for the gondola to descend — there is no free way down. Budget for this before starting.
Workaround: Buy the gondola descent ticket online before hiking — same price either way.

🌊 Claude Inspiration

Vancouver is the only major city in the world where you can ski a glaciated mountain in the morning and kayak an ocean fjord in the afternoon — and both are within 30 minutes of downtown towers. What makes it remarkable isn't any single attraction but the improbability of the combination: a genuinely great city pressed right up against genuinely wild nature, with no buffer between them. The seawall wraps around a rainforest. The gondola summit looks down on a financial district. The suspension bridge crosses a canyon you can't see the bottom of. Everywhere you go, the scale of the landscape makes the city feel provisional — like it arrived recently and hasn't fully decided whether to stay. That tension is Vancouver's real quality.
