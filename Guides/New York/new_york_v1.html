Day 1
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚕 20 min → 9/11 Memorial & Museum
1. 9/11 Memorial & Museum
📒 The twin reflecting pools — the largest man-made waterfalls in North America — mark the footprints of the original towers. The museum beneath traces the attacks and their aftermath through 40,000 artifacts and 23,000 photos with unflinching honesty. A profound, necessary stop.
📖 Wikipedia

🚶 3 min → One World Observatory
2. One World Observatory
📒 The tallest building in the Western Hemisphere rises 1,776 ft — the symbolic height intentional. The observatory on floors 100–102 delivers a 360° panorama that spans five states. A sky-rise experience with genuine emotional weight given the site it stands on.
📖 Wikipedia

3. Brooklyn Bridge
📒 A nineteenth-century suspension bridge — a cathedral of cables and granite towers — remains one of the great civic achievements of any age. Walk the elevated wooden pedestrian path from Manhattan to Brooklyn for skyline views that improve with every step. The best view of Lower Manhattan is looking back mid-span.
📖 Wikipedia
🏛️ Open 24/7

4. Brooklyn Heights Promenade
📒 A half-mile esplanade cantilevered over the BQE delivers the single best unobstructed view of the Manhattan skyline — the Twin Towers once dominated it, and One World Trade has reclaimed that anchor. The Victorian brownstones of Brooklyn Heights behind you; the whole island spread before you.
📖 Wikipedia

Day 2
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 18 min · 🚕 8 min → The Frick Collection
1. The Frick Collection
📒 Henry Clay Frick's gilded-age Fifth Avenue mansion houses one of the world's most intimate Old Master collections — Vermeer's Officer and Laughing Girl, Rembrandt's Self-Portrait, Velázquez's Philip IV in a single room. Recently reopened after a landmark renovation, now with a stunning new garden court.
📖 Wikipedia

🏛️ Mon · Wed–Sun 10:30am–5:30pm
🚫 Closed Tuesday
⏰ ~1 hr 30 min
📍 1 E 70th St · Upper East Side
🚶 15 min · 🚕 5 min → Metropolitan Museum of Art
2. Metropolitan Museum of Art
📒 One of the world's great encyclopedic museums — two million objects across five thousand years of human civilization under a single roof. The Egyptian wing's Temple of Dendur, transported from Nubia before the Aswan flood, glows in late-afternoon light. Set priorities before you enter; this building rewards a slow visit, not a sprint.
📖 Wikipedia

🏛️ Sun–Tue · Thu 10am–5pm · Fri–Sat 10am–9pm

🚶 12 min · 🚕 5 min → Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum
3. Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum
📒 Frank Lloyd Wright.s iconic spiral building remains one of the great architectural statements of the twentieth century — the continuous spiral ramp is the exhibit as much as what hangs on it. The collection runs from Kandinsky and Picasso to contemporary commissions. Walk down from the top for the best experience.
📖 Wikipedia

Day 3
🏨 FROM HOTEL: 🚶 5 min · 🚕 4 min → Museum of Modern Art
1. Museum of Modern Art
📒 MoMA's permanent collection is a condensed history of modern art across the past century and a half — Van Gogh's Starry Night, Monet's Water Lilies panels, Picasso's Les Demoiselles d'Avignon, Warhol, Pollock — in one building. The sculpture garden, open to the sky between the East and West Wings, is one of Manhattan's great civic spaces.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟️ MoMA General Admission · moma.org
🏛️ Mon–Thu · Sat–Sun 10:30am–5:30pm · Fri 10:30am–8:30pm
⏰ ~2 hr
📍 11 W 53rd St · Midtown
🚶 7 min · 🚕 4 min → Top of the Rock
2. Top of the Rock
📒 Rockefeller Center's 70th-floor observation deck offers what many consider the best skyline photograph in New York — the Empire State Building dead-center against Midtown, with Central Park stretching north behind it. Three levels, the top fully open-air. Book the sunset time slot for the most dramatic light.
📖 Wikipedia
🎟️ Top of the Rock General Admission · rockefellercenter.com
🏛️ Daily 8am–midnight
⏰ ~1 hr
📍 30 Rockefeller Plaza · Midtown
🚕 12 min → High Line
3. High Line
📒 A decommissioned freight rail line elevated above the Chelsea streetscape, transformed into a 1.45-mile linear park with meadow plantings, public art installations, and Hudson River views. Walk south from 34th Street to Gansevoort — the route passes the Hudson Rail Yards and narrows to a glass-floored walkway over 10th Avenue.
📖 Wikipedia
🏛️ Apr–Nov 7am–11pm · Dec–Mar 7am–7pm
🆓 Free
⏰ ~1 hr 30 min
📍 820 Washington St · Chelsea
🚶 8 min → Edge at Hudson Yards
4. Edge at Hudson Yards
📒 The highest outdoor sky deck in the Western Hemisphere — 1,131 feet up on the 100th floor of 30 Hudson Yards — features a glass-floored platform suspended in mid-air, angled glass walls on three sides, and 360° views across the Hudson River and five states. The sensation of floating over Manhattan is genuinely exhilarating.
📖 Wikipedia

1. Brooklyn Museum
📒 The second-largest art museum in the United States — and far less crowded than the Met — houses 1.5 million objects including one of the world's finest Egyptian collections, the Elizabeth A. Sackler Center for Feminist Art (permanent home of Judy Chicago's The Dinner Party), and American decorative arts across full period rooms.
📖 Wikipedia

2. Brooklyn Botanic Garden
📒 Fifty-two acres of meticulously curated gardens including the Japanese Hill-and-Pond Garden — one of the oldest Japanese-inspired gardens in the United States, with a floating torii gate and a wooden viewing pavilion. The Cherry Esplanade hosts one of New York's most celebrated spring displays.

3. Prospect Park
📒 Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux considered this 585-acre Brooklyn park their finest work — a more naturalistic landscape than their Manhattan project, with a real forest, a glacial lake, and the Long Meadow, one of the longest unbroken lawns in any American park. Brooklyn's living room; its rhythms feel genuinely local.
📖 Wikipedia

📒 The grand neoclassical building at the top of the Benjamin Franklin Parkway — the "Rocky Steps" — houses 240,000 objects including the world's largest Duchamp collection, outstanding Impressionist paintings, and Japanese and Chinese temple rooms. The Parkway view from the top step is Philadelphia's defining vista.

3. Eastern State Penitentiary
📒 Eastern State pioneered solitary confinement under a radical philosophy of rehabilitation through silence. Al Capone served time here. Today the crumbling cellblocks — roofless, overtaken by plants, deeply atmospheric — constitute one of America's most extraordinary historic sites.

🍮 Local Tastes
New York–Style Pizza
A wide, thin, foldable slice with a crisp-then-chewy crust — baked in a coal or gas deck oven, eaten standing at the counter. The style evolved from Neapolitan in the early 1900s and is now legally inseparable from the city itself.
📍 Joe's Pizza · Di Fara (Brooklyn) · Prince Street Pizza
New York Bagel
The bagel's texture — chewy inside, glossy crust — comes from being boiled in water before baking, a technique brought by Eastern European Jewish immigrants. The New York water is widely credited (if unverified) for the result. Eat with lox and cream cheese.
📍 Russ & Daughters · Absolute Bagels · Ess-a-Bagel
NYC Pastrami on Rye
Beef brisket cured in salt and spices, then smoked and steamed — piled on seeded rye with brown mustard. The Lower East Side Jewish deli tradition at its apex. Order it hand-sliced, never the machine-cut version.
📍 Katz's Delicatessen · 2nd Avenue Deli
New York Cheesecake
Dense, rich, and creamy — the New York style uses cream cheese, heavy cream, and a graham cracker crust. Nothing subtle about it, and that's the point. Best eaten at a diner counter on a weekday morning.
📍 Junior's (Brooklyn original) · Eileen's Special Cheesecake
